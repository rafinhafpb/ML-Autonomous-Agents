The project consists of using Reinforcement Learning methods to train and evaluate agents to play the 2-player board game Cathedral. The techniques used for training the agents include Deep Q-Network (DQN) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The environment construction for the board game was based on a customized PettingZoo environment, while the reward function was designed by the authors. The main findings of the work involve evaluating and comparing the models regarding their best performance, as well as analyzing the impacts of learning when trained with methods such as Curriculum Learning and Representation Learning. The main challenges of the study include ensuring the models are actually learning the rules of the game, finding optimal hyperparameters for stability and good generalization in the case of the DQN agent, and developing a good strategy for self-play improvement in the case of the PPO agent.

Regarding the issue pointed out in IV. Results and Discussion - Fig. 3., where the DQN model performs better at the beginning and then converges to a state where it draws every game, hyperparameter tuning may help stabilize the performance (mostly by adjusting the learning rate across episodes), but it may not completely solve the problem. Hyperparameter optimization with Optuna (Lecture 6) could be a good starting point, but they might also consider increasing the complexity of the reward function and adding a modest penalty when the model draws a game (instead of rewarding 0). The reward function could be tuned, for example, to become a weighted sum of three strategies:

- Area Control Evaluation: the ratio between controlled squares and the total playable area.
- Blocking Assessment: how well a placement blocks an opponent's move or counters a potential threat.
- Piece Placement Quality: the already-used strategy, where the value of a piece is proportional to its size.

The weights contributing to the sum could also adjust the reward magnitude based on context, such as increasing the importance of Area Control in the early game, boosting the Blocking weight in the mid-game, and emphasizing Piece Quality in endgame positions. Modifying the reward function accordingly could also improve the performance of PPO agents, which are struggling to outperform a random policy agent, indicating instability in training.
